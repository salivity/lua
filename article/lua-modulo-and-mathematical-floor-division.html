Lua Modulo and Mathematical Floor Division

In the Lua programming language, the modulo operator (%) is defined using mathematical floor division rather than truncation towards zero. While languages like C99, C++, and Java round integer division toward zero, Lua aligns with Python and number theory by rounding the division quotient toward negative infinity. As a result, the remainder produced by Lua's modulo operation always takes the sign of the divisor, ensuring predictable mathematical continuity when working with negative values.

The Division Identity and Remainder

In computer science and mathematics, the relationship between a dividend \(a\), a divisor \(b\), a quotient \(q\), and a remainder \(r\) is governed by the division algorithm:

\[a = q \times b + r\]

The definition of the modulo operator depends entirely on how the quotient \(q\) is rounded:

  • Truncated Division (Rounding toward zero): The fractional part of \(a / b\) is simply dropped. For negative results, numbers round upward toward zero (e.g., \(-2.33\) becomes \(-2\)). Consequently, the remainder \(r\) retains the sign of the dividend \(a\).
  • Floored Division (Rounding toward negative infinity): The quotient is defined as \(q = \lfloor a / b \rfloor\). For negative results, numbers round downward away from zero (e.g., \(-2.33\) becomes \(-3\)). Consequently, the remainder \(r\) retains the sign of the divisor \(b\).

Lua's Formal Definition

The Lua reference manual explicitly defines the modulo operator as:

a % b == a - math.floor(a / b) * b

Because math.floor rounds to the largest integer less than or equal to the argument, Lua strictly implements Donald Knuth’s floored division.

Truncation vs. Floor Division by Example

Consider the operation -7 % 3:

  • In Truncated Division (e.g., C, Java):
    • \(q = \text{trunc}(-7 / 3) = \text{trunc}(-2.333...) = -2\)
    • \(r = -7 - (-2 \times 3) = -7 - (-6) = -1\)
  • In Lua's Floored Division:
    • \(q = \text{floor}(-7 / 3) = \text{floor}(-2.333...) = -3\)
    • \(r = -7 - (-3 \times 3) = -7 - (-9) = 2\)

Because \(b = 3\) is positive, the remainder in Lua is guaranteed to fall in the range \([0, 2]\).

Conversely, if the divisor is negative, such as 7 % -3:

  • \(q = \text{floor}(7 / -3) = \text{floor}(-2.333...) = -3\)
  • \(r = 7 - (-3 \times -3) = 7 - 9 = -2\)

The remainder shares the negative sign of the divisor, lying within the range \((-3, 0]\).

Advantages of Floor-Based Modulo

Lua’s decision to follow floored division provides notable mathematical and practical advantages:

  1. True Periodic Wrapping: When modeling cyclic systems—such as circular buffers, clock arithmetic, or wrapping coordinates in game development—a negative index naturally wraps around to the end of the range. In Lua, -1 % 10 evaluates directly to 9, whereas truncated division produces -1, which often requires an extra conditional check.
  2. Euclidean Invariance: In modular arithmetic, the identity \((a + k \times b) \pmod b = a \pmod b\) holds true for any integer \(k\). Floored division preserves this identity across the zero boundary, whereas truncated division breaks it when crossing from positive to negative dividends.
